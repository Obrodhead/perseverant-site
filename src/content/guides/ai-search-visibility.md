## Why does this matter now?

Prospects now ask AI assistants who to hire, and the answer often names two or three businesses and stops. If you are not one of them, you never hear about the question.

Google says AI Mode passed 1 billion monthly users (Sundar Pichai, Alphabet Q2 2026 earnings remarks, July 22, 2026). In BrightLocal's 2026 survey of 1,002 US adults, use of ChatGPT and other AI tools for local recommendations rose from 6% to 45% (BrightLocal, a local marketing software vendor, February 11, 2026).

The old click is shrinking. Pew Research Center found Google users clicked a traditional result on 8% of visits when an AI summary appeared, versus 15% without one (Pew, July 22, 2025). Being cited still helps: Seer Interactive, a marketing agency, found brands cited in an AI Overview earned 35% more organic clicks than brands not cited (Seer Interactive, November 4, 2025).

Ranking a link is no longer the whole job. You also need to be the name inside the answer.

<!-- industry:law-firms -->

## What this looks like for law firms

People with a legal problem now ask an AI assistant before they call anyone. In a 2026 survey of 1,110 US adults by iLawyer Marketing, a legal marketing agency, the share using ChatGPT to research attorneys rose from 28.1% to 41.9% in a year (iLawyer Marketing, 2026).

They rarely type "personal injury lawyer." They describe what happened. The assistant explains the process, then suggests a few firms. Test both halves: is the explanation right for your state, and are you named?

Your fixes run into bar rules. ABA Model Rule 7.1 bars misleading communications, and "specialist" claims generally need a recognized certification. Some states review ads; Florida requires most to be filed 20 days before first use, though websites are exempt. Explain process, timelines and fees plainly, with no outcome promises. This is not legal advice.

## What this looks like for elective practices

Elective patients research for weeks before they book, and more of that research now happens in a chat window. The questions are about cost, candidacy and risk. The assistant explains, then names a few practices.

Demand is there. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons reported cosmetic surgical procedure volume rose 7% in 2025 over 2024 (ASPS, September 1, 2026). More practices compete for the same patient, so the short list matters.

Give the assistant honest material to quote: real price ranges, the factors that move them, and the clinician who wrote or reviewed each page. Avoid before-and-after promises.

Keep patient information out of the whole process. HHS guidance still covers tracking on patient portals, scheduling pages and other pages that send health information (HHS OCR guidance, updated after the June 20, 2024 court ruling). This is not legal advice.

## What this looks like for home improvement companies

Your buyers ask AI assistants at two moments: right after something breaks, and while they plan a bigger project. Both end with a short list of companies.

Location carries more weight for you than for most industries. Google's local guidance names relevance, distance and prominence as the main factors (Google Business Profile Help). Service-area pages with real town names, and correct details on the review sites the assistants cite, usually matter more than a long blog.

Cost pages are your strongest answer pages. "How much does a heat pump cost" with honest ranges and cost drivers gives an assistant something to quote. If you sell solar, disclose total cost and financing terms, and do not overstate savings or tax credits (FTC, August 7, 2024).

<!-- industry:senior-living -->

## What this looks like for senior living communities and home care agencies

The person asking is often an adult child, stressed and searching at night. The assistant explains the care options, then names a few providers. Plain pages on independent living, assisted living, memory care and in-home care, with honest cost ranges, are what it can quote.

Your sources column will probably show referral sites. One operator told Senior Housing News it received 465 referral-site leads in 2024 that produced 12 move-ins, and cancelled the contracts. Keep your details on those sites correct, and build your own pages so the assistant has a direct source.

With senior housing occupancy at 89.9% in Q2 2026 (NIC MAP, July 20, 2026), being named for the right care level in the right area matters more than raw volume.

<!-- industry:recruiting-staffing -->

## What this looks like for recruiting and staffing firms

Your buyer is an employer, and employers now ask AI assistants the same way consumers do. A plant manager asks which firms place a specific role in a region, and the assistant names a few.

Staffing Industry Analysts expects the US staffing market to grow 2.4% in 2026 (SIA, September 6, 2026). In a slow-growth market, showing up when an employer asks is a real edge.

Assistants match firms by role, sector and region, so a generic "we staff all industries" page gives them little. Build pages for the roles and regions you actually fill. Trade publications, business journals and association directories often appear in the sources column; those are the places to be quoted. Test employer prompts only, not job-seeker ones.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:estate-security -->

## What this looks like for estate security firms

Your buyers are private. Many will never call three firms to compare. They ask an assistant quietly and read the answer alone, so its explanation becomes part of your sales process.

ASIS International reports a professional threat assessment typically costs $25,000 to $50,000 (ASIS International, April 2025). Check whether the assistants quote reasonable ranges, and whether your pages explain what drives cost: scope, number of properties, travel and monitoring.

Keep client details off the site entirely. Give assistants things they can verify instead: team credentials, association memberships, published articles and a clear description of your process. In a niche market the sources column may be thin, which is an opening.

## What this looks like for your business

The pattern holds in most service businesses. A buyer describes a problem in plain words, the assistant explains it, then names a few businesses. Your audit checks both: whether the explanation is accurate, and whether you are one of the names.

## Step 1: What questions should you test?

Test the questions a buyer types right before they call, not the keywords you rank for. Write 12 prompts in plain language, with your city or a real situation in each.

| Prompt type | Template | How many | Your versions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who to hire | Who should I hire for [service] in [city]? | 3 | |
| Cost | How much does [service] cost in [city], and what drives the price? | 2 | |
| How to choose | What should I ask a [business type] before I hire them? | 3 | |
| Comparison | [Your business] vs [top competitor] for [situation]? | 2 | |
| Name check | What do you know about [your business] in [city]? Any concerns? | 2 | |

## Step 2: How do you run and score the prompts?

Run each prompt from a clean session, in each assistant, twice, and score every answer the same way. Your own history and location shape what you see, and answers vary between runs.

- [ ] Private window, logged out, or memory and personalization turned off
- [ ] City named in the prompt, not left to the browser
- [ ] ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, Perplexity, Claude and Gemini, web search on
- [ ] Every prompt run twice, with the text and date saved
- [ ] No client, patient or case details in any prompt

| Prompt # | Assistant | Run | Named? | Position | Linked to you? | Accurate? (note errors) | Competitors named | Sources cited |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ChatGPT | 1 | | | | | | |
| | | | | | | | | |

**Mention rate** = answers naming you ÷ total answers scored. Do the same for your top two competitors.

Example numbers, not benchmarks: 120 answers (12 prompts, 5 assistants, 2 runs), named in 18, is a 15% mention rate. A competitor named in 54 is at 45%.

The sources column matters most. It shows which directories, review sites and publications the assistants trust in your market. Those are the places you need to be.

## Step 3: Can AI crawlers actually read your site?

Check this before you write a new page. If crawlers cannot see your content, nothing else shows up in their answers. Vercel found that none of the major AI crawlers, including GPTBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot, render JavaScript; Gemini uses Googlebot's infrastructure and does (Vercel, December 17, 2024).

- [ ] "View page source" (not "Inspect") on your homepage and a key service page shows your main text, phone and address
- [ ] yoursite.com/robots.txt has no `Disallow` lines blocking OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Googlebot or Bingbot
- [ ] Site verified and sitemap submitted in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools

OpenAI says sites that block OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers, and that GPTBot, its training crawler, is controlled separately (OpenAI crawler documentation). You can block training and still allow search. Make that a deliberate choice, not an accident from an old plugin.

## Step 4: What should you fix first?

Fix fundamentals before buying any tool. We call this Search Essence Optimization: people search to understand a business, not just find it, and assistants work the same way. Meet them in the middle with one clear, consistent story.

Start with one sentence and use it everywhere: **[Business name] is a [business type] in [city or region] that helps [who] with [what problem], known for [one specific, verifiable difference].**

- [ ] **Consistent details.** Match name, address and phone across your site, Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect and directories. Fix every error from your accuracy column.
- [ ] **One page per real question.** Turn your weakest prompts into pages with a 40–70 word direct answer at the top.
- [ ] **Schema.** Organization or LocalBusiness markup, plus Person markup for owners, identical to the page.
- [ ] **Reviews.** Ask every satisfied customer and reply in your own words. BrightLocal found 47% of consumers won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews (BrightLocal, February 11, 2026). Never buy, fake or filter reviews; the FTC's 2024 rule bans them (Morgan Lewis, August 26, 2024).
- [ ] **Outside mentions.** Get listed or quoted on the sites in your sources column.

## Where do most owners get stuck?

> **One audit is a snapshot.** Answers change from run to run and week to week. A real picture takes repeated sampling across assistants, which is hard to keep up by hand.

> **Earning outside mentions.** Knowing which publications matter is the easy part. Getting featured takes relationships, pitches and content those editors want.

<!-- industry:law-firms -->

> **Regulated copy.** Answer pages need to help a scared prospect and still pass your state's advertising rules. Writing plainly about process and fees without drifting into outcome claims takes practice.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:elective-healthcare -->

> **Regulated copy and tracking.** Cost pages need to be honest without promising results, and the scheduling pages they lead to need tracking that keeps health information out of ad and analytics tools.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:home-improvement -->

> **Covering every town you serve.** Service-area pages only help if each says something true about that area. Writing a dozen without thin, copied text takes time most crews don't have.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:senior-living -->

> **Competing with referral sites.** Referral sites often sit above your own pages in the sources column. Building pages strong enough to be cited directly is slow work.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:recruiting-staffing -->

> **Mapping roles to pages.** You fill dozens of roles across sectors. Choosing which pages to build first, by margin and demand, takes data most firms don't pull together.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:estate-security -->

> **Proving expertise without clients.** Credibility has to come from credentials, published work and outside mentions. Building that record takes steady effort over months.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:other -->

> **Doing it every month.** The audit only pays off if you rerun it, compare results and keep fixing. Many owners run it once, then the business takes over.
