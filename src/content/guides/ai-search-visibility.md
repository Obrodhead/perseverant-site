## Why does this matter now?

Your prospects are asking AI assistants who to hire, and the answer often names two or three businesses and stops there. If you are not one of them, you may never know the question was asked.

The scale is real. Google says AI Mode passed 1 billion monthly users and that AI Mode and AI Overviews have been brought together (Sundar Pichai, Alphabet Q2 2026 earnings remarks, July 22, 2026). OpenAI says ChatGPT has more than 900 million weekly active users (OpenAI, February 27, 2026).

Local buyers are part of that shift. In BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey of 1,002 US adults, use of ChatGPT and other AI tools for local recommendations rose from 6% to 45% (BrightLocal, a local marketing software vendor, February 11, 2026).

The old click is shrinking. Pew Research Center found Google users clicked a traditional result on 8% of visits when an AI summary appeared, versus 15% without one, and clicked a link inside the summary on 1% of visits (Pew, July 22, 2025). Ahrefs found AI Overviews correlated with a 58% lower click-through rate for the top-ranking page between December 2023 and December 2025. That is correlation, not proof of cause (Ahrefs, an SEO software vendor, February 4, 2026).

Being cited still helps. Seer Interactive, a marketing agency, found brands cited in an AI Overview earned 35% more organic clicks than brands not cited, across 3,119 informational queries for 42 clients (Seer Interactive, November 4, 2025).

The takeaway is simple. Ranking a link is no longer the whole job. You also need to be the name inside the answer.

<!-- industry:law-firms -->

## What this looks like for law firms

People with a legal problem now ask an AI assistant before they call anyone. In a 2026 survey of 1,110 US adults by iLawyer Marketing, a legal marketing agency, the share using ChatGPT to research attorneys rose from 28.1% in 2025 to 41.9% in 2026, while Google fell from 86.7% to 71.9% (iLawyer Marketing, 2026).

They rarely type "personal injury lawyer." They type something closer to "I was rear-ended and the other driver's insurance keeps calling me, what should I do?" The assistant explains the process, then suggests a few firms. Your audit should test both halves: is the explanation accurate for your state, and are you one of the names?

The fixes run straight into bar rules. ABA Model Rule 7.1 bars false or misleading communications, and calling a lawyer a "specialist" generally requires a certification your state recognizes. So answer pages should explain process, timelines and fee arrangements in plain terms, with no outcome promises and no "expert" language. Some states also review advertising. Florida requires most ads to be filed at least 20 days before first use, though websites are exempt. Texas requires filing within 10 days of first use. Have every new page checked against your state's rules. This is not legal advice.

The usual frustration is simple. Another firm keeps getting named, and nobody on your team can say why. This audit is how you find out.

## What this looks like for elective practices

Elective patients research for weeks before they book, and more of that research now happens in a chat window. The questions are about cost, candidacy and risk: "How much do implants cost in my area?" "Am I a candidate for LASIK with dry eyes?" "How long is recovery from a facelift?"

Demand is there. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons reported cosmetic surgical procedure volume rose 7% in 2025 over 2024, with facelifts up 13% and patients 66 and older up 24% (ASPS, September 1, 2026). In dentistry, the ADA Health Policy Institute reports capacity is opening up and practices are less busy (ADA News, October 1, 2025). More open chairs means more practices competing for the same patient, and the assistant's short list matters more.

Your fixes should give the assistant honest material to quote. Publish real price ranges and the factors that move them. Name the clinician who wrote or reviewed each page, and match that in your Person schema. Avoid before-and-after promises.

Keep patient information out of the whole process. Never paste patient details into a prompt. HHS guidance still covers tracking technology on patient portals, scheduling pages and other pages that send health information, and vendors handling it need a business associate agreement (HHS OCR guidance, updated after the June 20, 2024 court ruling). This is not legal advice.

## What this looks like for home improvement companies

Your buyers ask AI assistants at two moments: right after something breaks, and while they plan a bigger project. The first sounds like "My furnace stopped working and it's 20 degrees, who can come today near Lakewood?" The second sounds like "Should I repair or replace a 20-year-old roof?" Both end with a short list of companies.

The market is steady, not booming. Harvard's Joint Center for Housing Studies projects owner-occupied improvement and repair spending will move from $509 billion in Q4 2025 to $518 billion in Q4 2026, growth of around 1.6% to 2.1% (Harvard JCHS, April 15, 2026). In a slower market, the companies that get named in the answer have an edge on the ones that only rank a link.

Location carries more weight for you than for most industries. Google's local guidance names relevance, distance and prominence as the main factors. Service-area pages with real neighborhood names, and correct details on the review and directory sites the assistants cite, usually matter more than a long blog.

Cost pages are your best answer pages. "How much does a heat pump cost" with honest ranges and the factors that change them gives an assistant something to quote. If you sell solar, keep savings claims in line with FTC guidance: disclose total cost and financing terms, and do not overstate savings or tax credits (FTC, August 7, 2024).

<!-- industry:senior-living -->

## What this looks like for senior living communities and home care agencies

The person asking is often not the person who will move in or receive care. It is a daughter or son, usually stressed, often at night, asking "How do I know when Mom needs memory care?" or "What does in-home care cost per hour in Ohio?" The assistant explains the options, then names a few providers.

That makes plain explanation your strongest asset. Pages that explain the difference between independent living, assisted living, memory care and in-home care, with honest cost ranges, are the kind of answer an assistant can quote.

The sources column in your audit will probably show referral sites. Watch it closely. One operator told Senior Housing News it received 465 referral-site leads in 2024 that produced 12 move-ins, and cancelled the contracts. If referral sites outrank your own pages in AI answers, make sure your details on them are correct, and build your own pages so the assistant has a direct source.

Context matters too. NIC MAP reports senior housing occupancy reached 89.9% in Q2 2026, with assisted living at 88.4% (NIC MAP, July 20, 2026). Home care faces a different squeeze: Activated Insights found 63.3% of providers turned down cases in 2023 because they could not staff them (Home Health Care News, August 2024). Either way, being named for the right care level in the right area matters more than raw volume.

## What this looks like for recruiting and staffing firms

Your buyer is an employer, and employers now ask AI assistants the same way consumers do. A plant manager types "Which staffing firms place CNC machinists in Northeast Ohio?" An HR director asks "Contract-to-hire or direct hire for a controls engineer?" The assistant answers, and it often names firms.

The market is growing slowly. Staffing Industry Analysts expects the US staffing market to grow 2.4% in 2026 to $183.1 billion (SIA, September 6, 2026). In a tight market, the firms that show up when an employer asks have a real advantage.

Assistants match firms to requests by role, sector and region. A generic "we staff all industries" page gives them little to work with. Pages for the specific roles and sectors you actually fill, with the regions you serve, help the assistant connect your firm to the request.

Outside mentions matter more here than in many consumer industries. Trade publications, local business journals and association directories often show up in the sources column. Those are the places to be listed or quoted.

Keep job seekers and employers apart in your audit. Job ads on Meta fall under the employment Special Ad Category, but this audit is about the employer who buys from you. Test employer prompts only.

<!-- industry:estate-security -->

## What this looks like for estate security firms

Your buyers are private, and they ask quietly. Many will never call three firms to compare. They will ask an assistant "How much does a threat assessment cost?" or "Who designs safe rooms for private homes in [region]?" and read the answer alone.

That makes the assistant's explanation part of your sales process. ASIS International reports a professional threat assessment typically costs $25,000 to $50,000 (ASIS International, April 2025). Check whether the assistants quote reasonable ranges, and whether your own pages explain what drives cost: scope, number of properties, travel, and ongoing monitoring.

You cannot lean on case studies or client names, and you should not try. Keep client details off the site entirely. Instead, give assistants things they can verify: the credentials of your team, association memberships, published articles, talks and a clear description of how your process works.

Your sources column may be thin. In a niche market, assistants may cite only a handful of sites, or answer from general knowledge. That is an opening. A few well-placed outside mentions, and a few pages that answer real buyer questions in plain terms, can move a small market more than a large one.

## What this looks like for your business

The pattern is the same in most service businesses. A buyer describes a problem in plain words, the assistant explains it, then names a few businesses. Your audit should check both: whether the explanation is accurate, and whether you are one of the names. Keep that in mind as you write your prompts.

## Step 1: What questions should you test?

Test the questions a buyer types right before they pick up the phone, not the keywords you rank for. Write 12 prompts across five types, in plain language, with your city or region in them.

| # | Prompt type | Template | Your version |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who to hire | Who should I hire for [service] in [city]? | |
| 2 | Who to hire | I need [service] near [neighborhood or suburb]. Who do people recommend? | |
| 3 | Who to hire | Which [business type] in [city] handle [specific situation]? | |
| 4 | Cost | How much does [service] cost in [city or state]? | |
| 5 | Cost | Is [service] worth the price, and what drives the cost up? | |
| 6 | How to choose | How do I choose a [business type] for [situation]? | |
| 7 | How to choose | What questions should I ask a [business type] before I hire them? | |
| 8 | How to choose | What are red flags when hiring a [business type]? | |
| 9 | Comparison | [Your business] vs [competitor]: which is a better fit for [situation]? | |
| 10 | Comparison | What's the difference between [option A] and [option B] for [need]? | |
| 11 | Name check | What do you know about [your business name] in [city]? | |
| 12 | Name check | Is [your business name] a good choice for [service]? Any concerns? | |

Write prompts the way a stressed buyer talks, with the situation in the sentence. A situation tests what the assistant understands. A bare keyword only tests what it can match.

## Step 2: How do you run the prompts cleanly?

Run each prompt from a clean session, in each assistant, twice. Your own history, location and logged-in account can shape what you see, and answers vary from one run to the next.

- [ ] Use a private or incognito browser window, logged out where the assistant allows it
- [ ] If you must log in, turn off memory or personalization, or use a fresh account
- [ ] Set or state the location. Put the city in the prompt rather than trusting the browser to guess
- [ ] Test ChatGPT, Google AI Mode and AI Overviews, Perplexity, Claude and Gemini
- [ ] Run every prompt twice, a few minutes apart, and record both answers
- [ ] Turn on web search where the assistant offers it, and note when it is off
- [ ] Save a screenshot or copy the text, with the date and time
- [ ] Do not paste client, patient or case details into any prompt

Expect differences. The same prompt can name different businesses on two runs. One run tells you little. Patterns across runs tell you a lot.

## Step 3: How do you score the answers?

Score every answer on the same five questions so you can compare assistants and track change over time. Use one row per prompt, per assistant, per run.

| Prompt # | Assistant | Run | Named? (Y/N) | Position (1st, 2nd, 3rd, not listed) | Cited with a link? (Y/N) | Accurate? (Y/N, note errors) | Competitors named | Sources cited |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ChatGPT | 1 | | | | | | |
| 1 | ChatGPT | 2 | | | | | | |
| 1 | Perplexity | 1 | | | | | | |

Then fill in the summary.

**Mention rate** = answers where you were named ÷ total answers scored.

**Citation rate** = answers that linked to your site ÷ total answers scored.

**Accuracy issues** = list every wrong fact: old address, wrong services, closed location, wrong owner name.

| Summary | You | Competitor A | Competitor B |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mention rate | | | |
| Citation rate | | | |
| Most-cited sources | | | |

The "sources cited" column is the most useful one. It shows which directories, review sites, publications and competitor pages the assistants trust in your market. Those are the places you need to be.

Example numbers, not benchmarks: if you scored 120 answers (12 prompts, 5 assistants, 2 runs) and were named in 18, your mention rate is 15%. If a competitor was named in 54, they are at 45%. The gap tells you where to start.

## Step 4: Can AI crawlers actually read your site?

Check this before you write a single new page. If crawlers cannot see your content, nothing else you do will show up in their answers. It takes about 20 minutes.

**The view-source test (10 minutes).** Most AI crawlers read only the raw HTML your server sends. Vercel found that none of the major AI crawlers, including GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot, render JavaScript. Gemini uses Googlebot's infrastructure and does (Vercel, December 17, 2024).

- [ ] Open your homepage and a key service page in a browser
- [ ] Right-click and choose "View page source" (not "Inspect")
- [ ] Search the source for a sentence from your main paragraph
- [ ] Search for your phone number and address
- [ ] If the text is missing from the source, crawlers that skip JavaScript probably cannot see it

**The robots.txt check (5 minutes).** Go to yoursite.com/robots.txt. Look for any `Disallow` lines that apply to these crawlers or to everyone (`User-agent: *`).

| Crawler | Who runs it | What it does | Allowed? |
|---|---|---|---|
| OAI-SearchBot | OpenAI | ChatGPT search answers | |
| GPTBot | OpenAI | Model training, controlled separately | |
| ClaudeBot | Anthropic | Claude | |
| PerplexityBot | Perplexity | Perplexity answers | |
| Googlebot | Google | Google Search, AI Overviews, AI Mode | |
| Bingbot | Microsoft | Bing search | |

OpenAI's documentation says sites opted out of OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers, and that GPTBot is controlled separately (OpenAI crawler documentation). You can block training and still allow search. Make that a deliberate choice, not an accident from an old plugin or a security setting.

**The Bing check (5 minutes).** Sign up for Bing Webmaster Tools, verify your site and submit your sitemap. Check that your key pages are indexed and look at any crawl errors.

- [ ] Main content appears in page source
- [ ] No unintended blocks in robots.txt
- [ ] Site verified in Bing Webmaster Tools and Google Search Console
- [ ] Sitemap submitted to both

## Step 5: What should you fix first?

Fix the fundamentals before buying any new tool. AI has to understand you before it can recommend you. That is the heart of what we call Search Essence Optimization: people search to understand a business, not just find it, and the assistants work the same way. Your job is to meet them in the middle with one clear, consistent story.

**Start with one sentence.** Write a single sentence that says who you serve, what you do and where. Use it, word for word or close to it, on your site, your Google Business Profile, directories and social profiles.

Fill-in formula: **[Business name] is a [business type] in [city or region] that helps [who] with [what problem], known for [one specific, verifiable difference].**

**Then work through this list in order.**

- [ ] **Consistent name, address and phone.** Match them exactly across your site, Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yelp and industry directories. Fix every error from your accuracy column.
- [ ] **One page per real question.** Turn your best-performing prompts into pages. Put a 40–70 word direct answer at the top, then the detail. A page that answers "how much does [service] cost in [city]" with real ranges and real cost drivers gives an assistant something to quote.
- [ ] **Schema.** Add Organization or LocalBusiness markup with your name, address, phone, hours and service area. Add Person markup for owners and key staff. Keep it identical to what the page says.
- [ ] **Reviews.** Ask every satisfied customer, and respond to every review in your own words. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses, 47% won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews, 89% expect owners to respond and 50% are put off by templated replies (BrightLocal, February 11, 2026). Never buy, fake or filter reviews. The FTC's 2024 rule bans fake reviews, including AI-generated ones, bought reviews and review suppression (Morgan Lewis, August 26, 2024).
- [ ] **Outside mentions.** Look at the "sources cited" column. Get listed, quoted or featured on the publications, associations and directories your buyers already read. One mention on a site the assistants cite in your market is worth more than many on sites they never use.

## Where do most owners get stuck?

> **One audit is a snapshot.** Answers change from run to run and week to week. A real picture takes repeated sampling across assistants over several weeks, which is hard to keep up by hand.

> **Tracing why a competitor wins.** The sources column shows what assistants cite, but working out which of those sources actually moves the answer takes comparison across many prompts and markets.

> **Earning outside mentions.** Knowing which publications matter is the easy part. Getting featured on them takes relationships, pitches and content those editors want.

<!-- industry:law-firms -->

> **Regulated copy.** Answer pages need to be useful to a scared prospect and still reviewable under your state's advertising rules. Writing plainly about process and fees without drifting into outcome claims takes practice.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:elective-healthcare -->

> **Regulated copy and tracking.** Cost and candidacy pages need to be honest without promising results, and the scheduling pages they lead to need tracking that keeps health information out of ad and analytics tools. Getting both right takes care.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:home-improvement -->

> **Covering every town you serve.** Service-area pages only help if each one says something true and specific about that area. Writing a dozen of them without thin, copied text takes time most crews don't have.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:senior-living -->

> **Competing with referral sites.** Referral sites often sit in the sources column above your own pages. Building pages strong enough to be cited directly, while keeping your referral listings accurate, is slow work.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:recruiting-staffing -->

> **Mapping roles to pages.** Your desk fills dozens of roles across several sectors. Deciding which role and region pages to build first, based on margin and demand, takes data most firms don't pull together.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:estate-security -->

> **Proving expertise without clients.** You can't publish case studies, so credibility has to come from credentials, published work and outside mentions. Building that record takes steady effort over months.

<!-- /industry -->

> **Doing it every month.** The audit only pays off if you rerun it, compare results and keep fixing. Many owners run it once, then the business takes over.
