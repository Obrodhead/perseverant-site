## How does Google decide who shows up in the map pack?

Google ranks local results mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and says there is no way to pay for a better local ranking (Google Business Profile Help). Fundamentals win.

- **Relevance**: how well your profile and site match the search. A category naming the exact service beats a broad one.
- **Distance**: how far you are from the searcher. You win by being strong enough to rank farther out.
- **Prominence**: how well known you are, from reviews, links, mentions and site strength.

This is Search Essence Optimization. People search to understand you, not just find you. Make the answer to "can I trust them?" clear, so prospect and business meet in the middle.

## What this looks like for law firms

Prospects now check you in more than one place. iLawyer Marketing, a legal marketing agency, surveyed 1,110 US adults and found the share using ChatGPT to research attorneys rose from 28.1% in 2025 to 41.9% in 2026. Your profile and pages must answer the same questions everywhere.

Ranking only matters if someone picks up. In a secret-shopper test of 500 firms, Clio found 48% could not be reached by phone (March 24, 2025). If nobody answers, the next firm in the pack gets the client.

Everything you publish is attorney advertising. ABA Model Rule 7.1 bars misleading communications, "specialist" claims need certification, and outcome promises are out. The Florida Bar requires most ads be filed at least 20 days before first use, though websites are exempt. This is not legal advice.

## What this looks like for elective practices

Elective patients research for weeks and compare. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons reported cosmetic surgical volume rose 7% in 2025 over 2024 (September 1, 2026). Procedure pages need substance: candidacy, recovery, honest cost ranges and who performs the work.

Privacy shapes local SEO too. HHS tracking guidance still covers patient portals, scheduling pages and other pages that transmit protected health information, even after a federal court vacated the part covering unauthenticated public pages (June 20, 2024). Vendors handling PHI need a business associate agreement. Keep patient information out of analytics, ads, review requests and replies. This is not legal advice.

## What this looks like for home improvement companies

The market is growing slowly, so every job is a share fight. Harvard's Joint Center for Housing Studies projects owner-occupied improvement and repair spending moving from $509 billion in Q4 2025 to $518 billion in Q4 2026 (April 15, 2026). When the pie barely grows, the map pack decides who gets the estimate.

Hide your address if customers don't come to you, and list only towns your crews actually drive to. Finished work, photographed with permission and tagged by town, makes honest local pages easy.

Solar has extra rules. The FTC has said sellers must disclose total cost and financing terms, not overstate savings or tax credits, and flagged "free solar" claims as a scam signal (August 7, 2024). This is not legal advice.

## What this looks like for senior living communities and home care agencies

The searcher is usually an adult child, searching by the parent's town. A daughter in Columbus needs you to show up for her mother's town and explain the neighborhood, nearby hospitals and the drive.

Owning that visibility reduces dependence on referral sites. Senior Housing News reported one operator's 465 referral-site leads in 2024 produced 12 move-ins. Your own leads come to you first, so answer fast: Bild & Co. mystery shops found 53% of web inquiries got no response within two hours (Senior Housing News, February 12, 2024).

Home care agencies should list only the areas they can staff. Activated Insights found 63.3% of providers turned down cases in 2023 because they couldn't staff them (Home Health Care News, August 2024). A client you can't serve becomes a bad review.

<!-- industry:recruiting-staffing -->

## What this looks like for recruiting and staffing firms

Your buyers are employers, and they rarely pick a staffing partner from the map pack. They search by specialty and city, then read about the recruiters.

Staffing Industry Analysts forecasts the US staffing market will grow 2.4% in 2026 to $183.1 billion (September 6, 2026). In a slow market, being the clear answer for "accounting recruiters in Cleveland" wins share without outspending national brands.

Your profile has two audiences: candidates review how they were treated, and hiring managers read those reviews. Give employers a page on which roles you fill, where, how you screen, and how fees and guarantees work. AI assistants quote plain answers like these.

<!-- industry:estate-security -->

## What this looks like for estate security firms

Your clients value discretion. A service-area profile with a hidden address usually fits, and many clients will never post a review. So prominence comes from your principals' credentials, published explanations of your process, and mentions from architects, builders and associations.

ASIS International reported that a professional threat assessment typically costs $25,000 to $50,000 (April 2025). Buyers at that level research carefully and look for judgment, not marketing.

Pages that explain what an assessment includes, how a safe room project runs alongside a build, and how you protect client information let a prospect understand you before revealing anything. Write plainly, without fear-based copy. Treat regions, not towns, as your local unit.

## What this looks like for your business

Whatever you sell, the pattern is the same. Pick the most specific category, list only the area you truly serve, and show proof a careful buyer can check. The rest of this playbook works for any high-value local service.

## Why are we ranking but not getting calls?

Usually one of four patterns:

- **Wrong searches.** Ranking for your own name or research terms produces few calls.
- **Small circle.** Map rank changes block by block. You may top the pack near your office and vanish two miles away.
- **Weak answer.** Few reviews, no photos or a slow mobile site loses to a more trustworthy competitor one spot lower.
- **AI summaries.** Pew Research Center found Google users clicked a traditional result on 8% of visits when an AI summary appeared, versus 15% without (July 22, 2025). Seer Interactive, an agency, found brands cited in an AI Overview earned 35% more organic clicks (November 4, 2025). Pages must rank and be quotable.

## Step 1: Complete your Google Business Profile

Your profile is the map pack listing. Finish this list, then review it quarterly.

- [ ] Choose the most specific **primary category** for your main service.
- [ ] Add **secondary categories** only for services you actually sell.
- [ ] Use your real business name, with no added keywords or towns.
- [ ] Show your address, or hide it and set **service areas** to the region you truly serve.
- [ ] Add every **service** with a short, plain description.
- [ ] Set regular and holiday **hours**.
- [ ] Write a plain **description**: who you serve, what you do, where.
- [ ] Replace stock images with real **photos**.
- [ ] Add a **UTM-tagged website link** (`?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp`). If you use call tracking, make the tracking number primary and your main number additional.
- [ ] Publish a **post** at least twice a month.

## Step 2: Build a review system you can defend

Reviews are the part of prominence you control most. BrightLocal's 2026 vendor survey of 1,002 US adults found 47% won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews (February 11, 2026). Pass 20, then match the count and recency of the top three in your pack.

Keep it honest. The FTC's review rule, finalized August 2024, bans fake or AI-generated reviews, buying reviews, incentives tied to positive sentiment, undisclosed insider reviews and suppression (Morgan Lewis, August 26, 2024).

- [ ] Ask **every** client at a set moment. No gating unhappy ones to a private form.
- [ ] Send a direct link by text or email, with consent, within a day of that moment.
- [ ] Never reward a positive review, and don't let staff or family review without disclosure.
- [ ] Reply to **every** review within a few days, written fresh. BrightLocal found 50% are put off by templated replies.
- [ ] Answer criticism calmly, once, offline if possible.

<!-- industry:law-firms -->

For a law firm, ask when the matter closes, not mid-case. In replies, never confirm the reviewer was a client or discuss their matter. Check your bar's testimonial rules.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:elective-healthcare -->

For an elective practice, ask at the final follow-up visit. Keep the request generic, with no procedure or appointment details. In replies, never confirm the reviewer is a patient or mention treatment.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:home-improvement -->

For a home improvement company, ask at the final walkthrough. Have the project lead ask in person, then send the link that evening, and ask permission to photograph the finished work.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:senior-living -->

For senior living, ask about a month after move-in; for home care, after the first month of service. Ask the adult child, who usually writes the review. In replies, never mention a resident's health, care needs or room.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:recruiting-staffing -->

For a staffing firm, ask hiring managers after a placement clears its guarantee period, and candidates after their first week, placed or not. In replies, never name the employer or discuss a candidate's pay or performance.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:estate-security -->

For an estate security firm, fewer public reviews is normal. Ask only clients who are comfortable, and suggest they leave out their name, location and property details. Never confirm a client relationship in a reply.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:other -->

Ask when the work is finished and the client sees the result. In replies, never share private details about the client or their job.

## Step 4: Run the 15-minute technical health check

Content can't rank on a site Google can't read.

- [ ] Search `site:yourdomain.com`. The count should roughly match your real pages.
- [ ] Check why pages aren't indexed in Search Console's **Pages** report.
- [ ] Run your home page and top service page through **PageSpeed Insights** on mobile.
- [ ] Check that your top ten pages each have a unique **title tag** naming the service and place, and one H1.
- [ ] Test **schema** with Google's Rich Results Test. Name, address, phone and hours should match the profile.
- [ ] Confirm `robots.txt` doesn't block search or AI crawlers by accident.

Every failure goes on your fix list.

## Step 5: Measure calls and booked consultations, not rankings

Rankings are a means; booked appointments are the result.

**Cost per booked appointment from SEO = monthly SEO spend ÷ appointments booked from search and maps.**

Example numbers, not benchmarks: $3,000 a month producing 15 booked appointments is $200 each. Compare that with bought leads on the same basis. Bought leads start fast and stop when you stop paying; SEO starts slower and keeps working. Many owners do both.

## Where do most owners get stuck?

> **Seeing the whole map.** One search from your desk shows one point. Knowing your rank across your service area takes map-grid data collected repeatedly over time.

> **Content and links at scale.** Dozens of substantive, compliant pages plus mentions from local press and associations take steady time most owners don't have.

<!-- industry:law-firms -->

> **Answering the calls the map earns.** More visibility means more intake calls at odd hours. Without a tested intake process, the ranking work leaks out through voicemail.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:elective-healthcare -->

> **Tracking without PHI.** Knowing which search led to a booked consultation, without sending patient information to analytics or ad tools, takes careful setup on your scheduling pages and vendor agreements.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:home-improvement -->

> **Keeping proof current.** Town pages stay strong only if crews photograph finished jobs and someone files them by town and trade. That habit is hard to build in a busy season.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:senior-living -->

> **Turning searches into tours.** A family that finds you at 9 p.m. needs a fast, human reply. Without follow-up that covers evenings and weekends, the visibility goes to waste.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:recruiting-staffing -->

> **Separating the two audiences.** Candidate traffic often swamps employer traffic. Without clean tracking, it is hard to see whether search produces client conversations or only applicants.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:estate-security -->

> **Prominence without public proof.** When clients won't leave reviews and projects can't be shown, earning trust through credentials, partners and published expertise takes deliberate, slow work.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:other -->

> **Technical fixes on your CMS.** Knowing a page is not indexed is different from fixing the template, redirect or plugin that caused it. That work often needs a developer.
