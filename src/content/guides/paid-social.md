Many owners who say Facebook ads "don't work" are judging them on the wrong number. A cheap lead goes to voicemail, so the channel looks like junk. An expensive lead gets the campaign switched off before it books anyone.

This guide gives you a better scoreboard: the most you can pay for a booked appointment. Everything else is built to hit it.

## Step 1: What can you afford to pay for a booked appointment?

Your ceiling comes from your own numbers, not an industry average. Use the last 12 months. Where you don't track a number, use an honest guess and mark it.

| Line | Your number |
|---|---|
| Average first-year value of a new client | $ |
| × Gross margin (%) = value of a new client | $ |
| × Close rate from a held appointment (%) = value of a held appointment | $ |
| × Show rate (%) = value of a booked appointment | $ |
| × Share you'll spend on acquisition (%) | |
| **= Maximum cost per booked appointment** | **$** |

Use first-year value, not lifetime value. It keeps you safe while you learn what Meta leads actually do.

**A worked example.** Example numbers, not benchmarks.

- $8,000 first-year value × 50% margin = $4,000 per new client
- $4,000 × 25% close rate = $1,000 per held appointment
- $1,000 × 70% show rate = $700 per booked appointment
- $700 × 30% spend share = **$210 maximum cost per booked appointment**

Now work backward. If 40% of leads book, this business can pay up to $84 per lead. If only 10% book, it needs leads under $21. A $30 lead that books half the time is cheaper than a $12 lead that almost never does.

## What this looks like for law firms

A bad Meta campaign costs a law firm more than wasted spend. It costs a paralegal's afternoon calling accidental form fills while someone with a real matter waits.

Prospects also check you out before they answer your call. In a 2026 survey of 1,110 US adults by iLawyer Marketing, a legal marketing agency, the share using ChatGPT to research attorneys rose from 28.1% to 41.9% in a year. Your ad, site and reviews need to tell the same plain story about who you help.

Offers must stay inside advertising rules: no promised results, and no "specialist" or "expert" label without recognized certification. Texas extended its barratry law to social media and electronic messages (Texas HB 2733, effective September 1, 2025), so treat every follow-up text with the same care as the ad. Keep case facts off the form. They come out on the call.

<!-- industry:elective-healthcare -->

## What this looks like for elective practices

Elective patients are interested but rarely in a hurry. Someone weighing implants, a facelift or LASIK may watch your videos for months. A campaign works when it offers a low-pressure next step and someone answers quickly when they take it.

Demand is there. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons reported cosmetic surgical procedure volume rose 7% in 2025, with facelifts up 13% (ASPS, September 1, 2026).

Meta makes it harder than it used to be. Its 2025 restrictions on health and wellness advertisers can block optimization toward lower-funnel events such as scheduling, while in-app lead forms were affected least (Search Engine Land, March 11, 2025). That makes Instant Forms a practical start.

Privacy is the line you don't cross. Ask about timing and interest, never conditions or history. Keep anything that could be health information out of Meta, analytics and texts.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:home-improvement -->

## What this looks like for home improvement companies

Home improvement leads go cold fast. A homeowner with a leaking roof or dead furnace often requests several quotes in one evening, and the first real person to call back gets the first conversation. A good campaign still loses if the phone sits until morning.

The market is steady, not booming. Harvard's Joint Center for Housing Studies projects owner-occupied improvement and repair spending to grow from $509 billion to $518 billion between late 2025 and late 2026 (Harvard JCHS, April 15, 2026). In a flat market, you win by converting more of the leads you already pay for.

A free estimate is the natural offer. A location question saves wasted drives, and a project-type question routes roofing, HVAC and window leads to the right person. Ads that lead with financing need extra care, and solar needs the most (see Step 4).

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:senior-living -->

## What this looks like for senior living communities and home care agencies

The person filling out your form is often an adult child researching for a parent, squeezed between work and family. Your ad, form and first call should tell that person what the next step involves and that a real human will help.

Occupancy is high. NIC MAP reported senior housing occupancy at 89.9% in Q2 2026 (NIC MAP, July 20, 2026). Communities near full need fewer, better-fit leads. Communities with openings usually need steadier follow-up more than more volume.

Home care has a different limit. Activated Insights reported that 63.3% of providers turned down cases in 2023 because they couldn't staff them (via Home Health Care News, August 2024). Don't buy leads you can't serve.

Meta targeting is restricted for housing, so the message has to do the targeting. A tour or a care needs conversation usually beats a brochure.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:recruiting-staffing -->

## What this looks like for recruiting and staffing firms

Your buyer is an employer, not a job seeker. Owners and hiring managers scroll Facebook and Instagram on their own time, so your ad has to catch a business problem in a personal moment: a role open too long, a team stretched thin, a busy season coming.

Growth is modest. Staffing Industry Analysts expects the US staffing market to grow 2.4% in 2026, to $183.1 billion (SIA, September 6, 2026). In a slow market, new accounts go to firms that follow up well, not the ones that spend the most.

Keep employer campaigns and job ads apart, because only job ads fall under Meta's employment rules (see Step 4). Qualify on company size, roles and hiring timeline. A hiring needs call is the most direct offer. Automate the admin, but keep the first conversation human.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:estate-security -->

## What this looks like for estate security firms

Your prospects value privacy above almost everything. Volume will be low, a good month may bring a handful of serious inquiries, and every one deserves a fast, careful response.

The stakes are high. ASIS International notes that a professional threat assessment typically costs $25,000 to $50,000 (ASIS, April 2025). People weighing that kind of decision expect a confidential first step, not a hard sell.

Ask for as little as possible. Never ask about the property, family, travel or existing systems in an ad platform's form, and never name a neighborhood or imply you know who is looking. Favor calm, specific explanations of what a walkthrough or safe room involves over fear-based hooks. Prospects are searching to understand whether you are discreet and competent. Show them both before the first call.

<!-- industry:other -->

## What this looks like for your business

Whatever you sell, three questions decide a Meta campaign. Can you afford the leads it brings? Is the offer worth a stranger's phone number? Will someone call within minutes? The third is easy to overlook, and no amount of ad spend fixes a phone that nobody answers.

Step 1 answered the first. The steps below cover the other two, plus the special rules Meta applies to housing, employment, credit, health and legal advertising. If your business touches any of those, check Step 4 before you build anything. Getting a category wrong can get ads rejected or an account restricted, and it is far easier to set up correctly than to fix later.

## Step 2: Which offer is worth raising a hand for?

Pick one offer per campaign. It should match how ready the prospect is and never promise a result.

|---|---|---|
| Free case review | Personal injury, criminal defense | No case value or likely result |
| Estate planning workshop | Estate planning | Educational, no tax promises |
| "What to do after an accident" checklist | Personal injury | Practical steps, not case advice |
| Consultation with a named attorney | Any practice area | No "specialist" unless certified |

## Step 3: Lead form or landing page?

Start with Meta's Instant Form set to Higher intent, add one or two multiple-choice qualifying questions, and move to a landing page only if lead quality stays low. Higher intent adds a review screen before submitting. It costs some volume and screens out some accidental taps.

| Option | Use it when | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Instant Form, More volume | Your team can call everyone fast | Accidental submissions, wrong numbers |
| Instant Form, Higher intent | You want fewer, more deliberate leads | Slightly higher cost per lead |
| Either form plus qualifying questions | You need to route by location, timeline or service | More than two questions hurts completion |
| Landing page | The offer needs explaining, or you want booking on the spot | Needs fast load, clean tracking, short form |

Ask only what the first call needs.

## Step 4: What are the rules for your category?

Check your category before you build. This is a factual summary, not legal advice.

<!-- industry:law-firms -->

- **Advertising rules.** No false or misleading ads (ABA Model Rule 7.1); specialist claims need certification.
- **Filing.** The Florida Bar requires most ads be filed at least 20 days before first use; Texas requires filing within 10 days of first use.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:elective-healthcare -->

- **Health and wellness.** Meta's 2025 restrictions can block optimization toward scheduling; lead forms were affected least (Search Engine Land, March 11, 2025).
- **HIPAA tracking.** Tracking on scheduling pages and other pages that transmit protected health information is still covered, and vendors handling it need a business associate agreement (HHS OCR guidance).

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:home-improvement -->

- **Credit.** Financing ads may fall under Meta's credit category: age fixed at 18–65+, gender and ZIP targeting restricted, no lookalikes (Meta Marketing API documentation).
- **Solar.** Disclose total cost and financing terms; don't overstate savings or tax credits (FTC, August 7, 2024).

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:senior-living -->

- **Housing.** Senior housing ads can fall under Meta's housing category: age fixed at 18–65+, gender and ZIP targeting restricted, no lookalikes (Meta Marketing API documentation).
- **55+ claims.** These depend on the Housing for Older Persons Act exemption (NAHB, November 30, 2021). Run caregiver job ads in separate campaigns.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:recruiting-staffing -->

- **Employment.** Job ads fall under Meta's employment category: age fixed at 18–65+, gender and ZIP targeting restricted, no lookalikes. Ads selling your service to employers generally don't, but check each campaign (Meta Marketing API documentation).

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:estate-security -->

- **Special Ad Categories.** Estate security rarely falls under housing, employment or credit. Recheck any ad that promotes financing (Meta Marketing API documentation).

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:other -->

- **Housing, employment, credit.** Age fixed at 18–65+, gender and ZIP targeting restricted, no lookalikes (Meta Marketing API documentation).
- **Health and wellness.** Meta's 2025 restrictions can block optimization toward scheduling; lead forms were affected least (Search Engine Land, March 11, 2025).

<!-- /industry -->

- **Texts and calls (everyone).** Prior express written consent is still required for autodialed or prerecorded marketing texts and calls, and since April 11, 2025 opt-outs by any reasonable means must be honored within 10 business days (Kelley Drye; Burr & Forman).
- **Testimonials (everyone).** The FTC's August 2024 rule bans fake, bought and undisclosed insider reviews (Morgan Lewis, August 26, 2024).

## Step 5: How do you test creative in 14 days?

Run three hooks across two formats, keep the offer, audience and form fixed, and judge on booked appointments. The hook, the first line or first two seconds, is usually your biggest lever.

| | Short video, talking to camera | Single image with text |
|---|---|---|
| Hook 1: the problem in the prospect's words | | |
| Hook 2: what happens at the appointment | | |
| Hook 3: a common mistake or myth | | |

- [ ] Days 1–7: Launch all six and leave them alone. Pause early only if an ad spends well past your target with zero leads.
- [ ] Days 8–14: Compare booked appointments by ad. Pause clear losers, keep the top one or two.
- [ ] Day 15: Write down what you learned and change one thing for the next round.

Cost per booked appointment decides. Cost per lead and click-through rate only help you diagnose.

## Step 6: What happens after someone fills out the form?

Call every lead within minutes and track each one to a result. This decides more than any ad does. In a Harvard Business Review data set of 1.25 million leads, firms that made contact within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify the lead as firms that waited longer (HBR, March 2011).

## Where do most owners get stuck?

> **Sending CRM outcomes back to Meta correctly.** Meta's Conversions API can learn from "booked" and "held" events, but only if lead IDs match, timing is right and nothing sensitive goes along. Get it wrong and Meta learns the wrong lesson.

> **Making enough creative to beat fatigue.** Winning ads wear out. Most small teams run out of time for new hooks before the campaign runs out of steam.

<!-- industry:law-firms -->

> **Keeping every ad reviewable.** Each new hook may need checking or filing under your state's rules, and tracking versions and approvals often falls on the attorney.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:elective-healthcare -->

> **Measuring without exposing patient data.** Linking a lead to a held consultation without leaking health information takes careful setup and a recheck after every website change.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:home-improvement -->

> **Covering the phone after hours.** Homeowners fill out forms at night and on weekends. Reaching them within minutes is a staffing and routing problem before it's a marketing one.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:senior-living -->

> **Sustaining a long follow-up.** Aline, a senior living CRM vendor, reported a move-in takes about 28 sales activities (Aline, March 4, 2025). Keeping that personal and on schedule takes a cadence and an owner.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:recruiting-staffing -->

> **Reaching the person who decides.** Meta brings office managers, job seekers and owners in the same week. Getting the hiring decision-maker on a call takes routing most firms build by trial and error.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:estate-security -->

> **Keeping lead data private.** A list of people asking about safe rooms is sensitive in itself. Who sees it, where it lives and what goes back to ad platforms needs a deliberate answer.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:other -->

> **Reading results with small numbers.** Ten booked appointments can look like a trend when it's noise. Knowing when to wait, cut or change the offer comes from seeing many campaigns.
