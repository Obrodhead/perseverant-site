## Is an event right for your business?

An event is worth the effort when the decision is large, the buyer wants to learn before talking to anyone, and you have someone who can teach in plain language. People are not only searching to find you; they are searching to understand you. A good event lets them do that in an hour.

- [ ] Buyers hesitate for weeks or months because the decision costs or matters a lot.
- [ ] You have a presenter who explains clearly and does not oversell.
- [ ] You can name one next step, such as a consultation, a home assessment or a tour.
- [ ] Someone can call every attendee within a day or two.

All four checked means an event is worth a trial. Otherwise, fix the gaps first.

<!-- industry:law-firms -->

## What this looks like for law firms

Estate planning and family law seminars fit well. The questions are personal, and many people want to learn privately before they sit across a desk from a lawyer.

Research habits are shifting too. In a 2026 survey of 1,110 US adults by iLawyer Marketing, a legal marketing agency, the share using ChatGPT to research attorneys rose from 28.1% to 41.9% in a year. A summary can explain a trust. It cannot show how the lawyer who would handle the matter thinks. A seminar can.

The weak point is intake afterward. In a secret-shopper test of 500 firms in Clio's Legal Trends client intake guide (March 24, 2025), 48% could not be reached by phone. An attendee who calls the next morning and hits voicemail is a seat you paid for and lost.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:elective-healthcare -->

## What this looks like for elective practices

Implant, LASIK, orthodontic and cosmetic seminars let patients meet the doctor and hear the whole process before booking a consultation. These are elective, often self-paid decisions, and people want recovery, cost and risk explained plainly first.

Interest is there. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons reported on September 1, 2026 that cosmetic surgical procedure volume rose 7% in 2025. On the dental side, the ADA Health Policy Institute reported in October 2025 that capacity is opening up and dentists are less busy. An event can fill open chair time with people who already understand the treatment.

The constraints are about data, not demand. Use a registration form that asks for contact details only, reminder texts that never name a procedure, and ad audiences never built from patient lists.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:home-improvement -->

## What this looks like for home improvement companies

Storm, solar and remodel seminars work when they answer a real cost question. Homeowners make these purchases rarely and want to understand them before anyone comes to quote.

Harvard's Joint Center for Housing Studies projected on April 15, 2026 that owner-occupied improvement and repair spending would grow only about 1.6% to 2.1%, to $518 billion by Q4 2026. Slow growth means homeowners compare carefully. A contractor who teaches the buying process gives them a reason to call that one first.

Explain how storm claims work without promising approval. Walk remodel audiences through scope, permits, timeline and comparing bids. Hold events at your showroom or a community room in your service area, and make the next step a free home assessment, booked before another estimator is in the driveway.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:senior-living -->

## What this looks like for senior living communities and home care agencies

Family information sessions work because the decision usually rests with adult children, often on a deadline after a fall or hospital stay. They want costs, levels of care and the right questions before they tour. Host in your community so the event doubles as a tour; home care agencies can use a library or senior center.

Space is tight. NIC MAP reported senior housing occupancy of 89.9% in Q2 2026 (July 20, 2026). A steady information series lets you meet families early, before they choose under pressure.

Follow-up is where families are lost. In Bild & Co. mystery shops reported by Senior Housing News (February 12, 2024), 53% of web inquiries got no response within two hours. Ask care managers and discharge planners to share your invitation, too.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:recruiting-staffing -->

## What this looks like for recruiting and staffing firms

Employer-side webinars fit busy plant, operations and HR managers who will not drive across town but will join 40 minutes from their desk. The topic is their problem, not your service.

There is fresh material. Staffing Industry Analysts forecast on September 6, 2026 that the US staffing market will grow 2.4% in 2026 to $183.1 billion. A market-outlook webinar built on current data gives a hiring manager a reason to attend and to forward it to a peer.

Many registrants will watch the replay instead. Treat replay viewers as warm and count them separately. Make the next step a 30-minute hiring plan review, offered by a person, not just an automated email.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:estate-security -->

## What this looks like for estate security firms

Small, invitation-only briefings suit this audience better than open seminars. Owners of large homes and high-profile families are careful about who knows their address or concerns, so a public sign-up page can work against you.

Host a small group at a private venue or partner's office. Pass invitations through estate attorneys, architects and builders who already serve these families, with a short note they can forward as is.

The trust bar is high. ASIS International noted in April 2025 that a professional threat assessment typically costs $25,000 to $50,000. Nobody signs that after one ad. A briefing shows how you think and handle discretion before they invite you onto the property. Collect only name, email and phone, keep reminders neutral ("your Thursday briefing"), and never promise any measure will keep a family safe.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:other -->

## What this looks like for a high-value service business

The pattern holds across industries. Pick the question your buyers ask before they are ready to talk, teach it honestly in an hour, and make the next step clear. Check whether your field has its own advertising rules, as law and healthcare do, and whether your ads fall under one of Meta's Special Ad Categories for housing, employment or credit. Then put most of your effort into follow-up, because that is where booked consultations come from.

## Should it be in person, a webinar or both?

Go in person when trust is the main barrier and the audience is local. Go webinar when the audience is spread out or busy. Choose hybrid only if you can staff two rooms at once.

| Factor | In person | Webinar | Hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Builds trust | Fastest | Moderate | Strong in the room only |
| Cost per attendee | Higher | Lower | Highest to run well |
| Biggest risk | Empty seats you paid for | Low live attendance | Neither audience gets full attention |

Judge any format by cost per booked consultation, not cost per registration.

## What does the 21-day timeline look like?

Promote two to three weeks out, as ON24, an enterprise webinar platform, advised on November 26, 2025. ON24 also advises reminders one week, one day and 15 minutes before; we add a confirmation and a morning-of message for local events.

| Day | Task | Done |
|---|---|---|
| −28 | Pick topic, date, venue or platform, and presenter | [ ] |
| −24 | Build the registration page (checklist below) and confirmation email with a calendar link | [ ] |
| −21 | Test reminders end to end; launch ads, email your list, send partners a blurb to forward | [ ] |
| −14 | Second email; review cost per registration and cut what is not working | [ ] |
| −7 | One-week reminder (email, text optional) with one thing they will learn | [ ] |
| −3 | Rehearse; confirm room, tech and staff | [ ] |
| −1 | One-day reminder (email and text) with time, place or link, parking or tech note | [ ] |
| 0 | Morning-of and 15-minute texts with the address or link; run the event | [ ] |
| +1 to +3 | 72-hour follow-up (below) | [ ] |

Registration page checklist:

- [ ] Headline that repeats the topic question, with date, time and place above the form
- [ ] Three bullets on what attendees will learn, and a short factual presenter bio
- [ ] Form: name, email, phone, and an unchecked text-consent box that says they can reply STOP
- [ ] A plain line that the event is educational and nobody will be pressured

<!-- industry:law-firms -->

Do not ask about the person's legal matter on the form. That belongs in a confidential consultation.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:elective-healthcare -->

Do not ask about health conditions or procedures, keep third-party tracking off scheduling pages, and never name a procedure in a reminder text.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:estate-security -->

Skip the address field and any question about security concerns. A private link shared by partners can replace a public page.

## How many registrations do you actually need?

Work backward from the seats you want filled.

Seats wanted ÷ show rate = registrations needed
Registrations needed ÷ page conversion rate = clicks needed
Clicks needed × cost per click = estimated ad budget

**Example numbers, not benchmarks.** For 30 people in the room at a 50% show rate, you need 60 registrations. At 15% page conversion, that is 400 clicks. At $2.50 per click, about $1,000 in ads.

For context, ON24 reported a 60% average registrant-to-attendee rate in its 2025 data (June 19, 2026). That is a vendor figure drawn mostly from B2B enterprise webinars, not a target.

Run the last line after every event. It tells you whether to keep going.

## What should happen during and after the event?

Teach first and name one next step. Answer the title question in the first ten minutes, leave 15 minutes for questions, and explain exactly what happens in the consultation, assessment or tour. End with a feedback card or poll: "Would you like someone to call you?" yes or no.

Then move fast. In Harvard Business Review research on online leads (March 2011), firms that contacted leads within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify them as firms that waited longer. Attendees are warmer than web leads, but the clock still applies.

| When | Attendees | Registered no-shows |
|---|---|---|
| Same day | Thank-you email with a summary and booking link | Replay or next date |
| Day 1 | Call everyone who said yes on the card | Text (with consent) offering the replay |
| Day 2 | Call remaining attendees once | Second email with one key point |
| Day 3 | Email anyone not reached with a booking link | Invite to the next event |

<!-- industry:law-firms -->

For law firms, skip the Day 2 calls: phone only attendees who asked for follow-up, and send everyone else the summary.

<!-- /industry -->

Track each attendee in one list: attended, wants a call, called, booked, became a client.

## What compliance items apply?

Check these before launch. This is general information, not legal advice.

<!-- industry:law-firms -->

- **Filing ads.** Florida requires most ads filed at least 20 days before first use (websites exempt), which is tight inside a 21-day window. Texas requires filing within 10 days of first use.
- **No implied results.** ABA Model Rule 7.1 bars misleading communications. No "specialist" or "expert" claims without recognized certification.
- **Solicitation.** Rule 7.3 limits live person-to-person solicitation, so call only attendees who asked. Texas HB 2733 (effective September 1, 2025) extends barratry law to social media and electronic messages.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:elective-healthcare -->

- **Tracking and PHI.** HHS guidance still covers tracking on portals, scheduling and other pages that transmit protected health information; vendors handling it need a business associate agreement.
- **Meta limits.** Meta's health and wellness restrictions can block optimizing toward scheduling, with in-app lead forms affected least (Search Engine Land, March 11, 2025).
- **Health claims.** Keep procedure and recovery claims factual. No promised results.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:home-improvement -->

- **Solar disclosures.** The FTC (August 7, 2024) expects total cost and financing terms disclosed, no overstated savings or tax credits, and no "free solar" language.
- **No outcome promises.** Do not promise a claim will be approved or a project will save a set amount.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:senior-living -->

- **Meta housing category.** Community ads can fall under Meta's housing Special Ad Category, which fixes age targeting at 18–65+ and restricts ZIP targeting (Meta Marketing API docs).
- **55+ claims.** These depend on the Housing for Older Persons Act exemption (NAHB, November 30, 2021).
- **No outcome promises.** Describe care factually.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:recruiting-staffing -->

- **Meta employment category.** Job ads fall under it; ads selling services to employers generally do not, but check each campaign.
- **No outcome promises.** Do not promise time-to-fill or placement results.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:estate-security -->

- **Data you collect.** Collect only what the event needs and store registrant lists like client files.
- **No outcome promises.** Do not claim any product or service guarantees safety.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:other -->

- **Industry rules.** Lawyers, health providers and other licensed professions have their own advertising rules.
- **Meta Special Ad Categories.** Housing, employment and credit ads fix age targeting at 18–65+ and restrict ZIP targeting (Meta Marketing API docs).

<!-- /industry -->

- **Texting.** The TCPA still requires prior express written consent for autodialed marketing texts. Since April 11, 2025, people can revoke by any reasonable means, such as replying STOP, and opt-outs must be honored within 10 business days (Kelley Drye; Burr & Forman).
- **Email.** CAN-SPAM covers B2B email too: accurate headers, a postal address and opt-outs honored within 10 business days (FTC). Bulk Gmail senders also need SPF, DKIM and DMARC (Google, February 1, 2024).

## Where do most owners get stuck?

> **Filling seats at a sane cost.** The first event shows your real cost per registration. Lowering it takes testing topics, audiences and creative across several events before the money runs out.

> **Reminder automation across email and text.** A sequence that pulls from your form, respects text consent, honors STOP and stops for cancellations takes systems work most teams do not have time for.

<!-- industry:law-firms -->

> **Intake that keeps up with the event.** One evening can produce a dozen consultation requests. Answering every call, running conflict checks and booking within a day takes an intake process few firms have tested under that load.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:elective-healthcare -->

> **Measuring results without patient data.** Proving which seminar produced which consultations, while keeping health details out of ad platforms and analytics, takes careful tracking design and a signed agreement with every vendor involved.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:home-improvement -->

> **Booking assessments before someone else does.** Attendees often invite several companies to quote. Getting on the calendar first means calling the next morning, with an estimator schedule that can absorb a busy week.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:senior-living -->

> **Staying in touch across a long decision.** Families may attend months before they are ready. Keeping that relationship warm without it feeling like a sales push takes a CRM set up for it and someone who owns it.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:recruiting-staffing -->

> **Turning registrants into live conversations.** Busy managers register, skip the live session and watch the replay later, if at all. Tracking replay views and following up with the right message takes systems most firms only half use.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:estate-security -->

> **Growing the invitation list without going public.** Referral partners are the channel, but keeping them engaged and tracking who came from whom takes steady work that rarely gets done.

<!-- /industry -->

<!-- industry:other -->

> **Attribution from seat to signed client.** Most owners know how many people registered. Few can tell which ad, topic or event produced the clients who signed three months later. That needs one connected record from click to close.

<!-- /industry -->
