## Clients decide fast, and they decide on trust

Someone who needs a lawyer is often stressed and short on time. They search, read reviews, ask an AI
assistant and contact more than one firm. The firm that explains things plainly and calls back first
usually gets the conversation. Good marketing makes that easy. Good intake keeps up with it.

## More people ask AI before they call

A 2026 survey of 1,110 U.S. adults by iLawyer Marketing, a legal marketing agency, found the share who
used ChatGPT to research attorneys rose from 28.1% to 41.9% in a year, while Google use fell from 86.7%
to 71.9% ([press release, 2026](https://napavalleyregister.com/online_features/press_releases/google-usage-for-attorney-research-drops-15-points-as-chatgpt-climbs-to-42/article_01abf5f7-54f7-5f3a-bd97-5d9d739b88bf.html)).
It's one agency's survey, so treat the numbers as direction, not gospel. The direction is clear enough.

An assistant can only describe your firm from what it can read: attorney bios, practice area pages,
reviews and profiles. When those are thin or inconsistent, it fills the gap with a guess, or with the
firm across town.

## Most cases are lost at the phone

In a secret-shopper test of 500 firms, Clio found 48% could not be reached by phone, and only 33% of
firms contacted by email responded ([Clio, March 24, 2025](https://www.clio.com/uk/guides/client-intake-legal-trends/)).
No campaign fixes that. The cheapest new case is often the one already calling.

## The rules shape the work

Attorney advertising follows state bar rules, most built on the ABA Model Rules: no misleading
statements, no paying for recommendations and no "specialist" claims without certification. Some states
add their own steps. The Florida Bar requires most ads to be filed for review at least 20 days before
first use, though websites are exempt ([Florida Bar](https://www.floridabar.org/ethics/etad/advertising-filing-requirements/)).
We draft for your review and never promise outcomes. This is not legal advice.
