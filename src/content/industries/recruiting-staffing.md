## Selling to hiring managers

Employers choose recruiting partners for specialization and proof they can fill hard roles. They want a
partner who already understands their market before the first meeting. Your content and campaigns should
show that knowledge, not just claim it.

## A slow market rewards specialists

Staffing Industry Analysts forecasts the U.S. staffing market to grow 2.4% in 2026, to $183.1 billion
([SIA, September 6, 2026](https://www.staffingindustry.com/research/research-reports/americas/us-staffing-industry-forecast-september-2026-update)).
In a market growing that slowly, the firms that win are the ones a hiring manager already knows and trusts
for a specific kind of role.

## Most staffing sites speak to candidates

Job seekers bring traffic, so many staffing sites are built almost entirely for them. Employers and the
AI assistants they ask need something different: pages for the industries and roles you fill, how you
screen, how fast you typically submit and what a partnership looks like. We build that side without
weakening the candidate side.

## Follow-up between hiring cycles

Bullhorn's 2025 survey of more than 1,500 recruitment professionals found firms focused on full-cycle
automation were more than twice as likely to see revenue growth ([Staffing Industry Analysts, March 25, 2025](https://www.staffingindustry.com/editorial/engineering-staffing-report/staffing-firms-using-automation-twice-as-likely-to-grow-revenue-study-finds)).
It's a vendor survey, and the lesson is simple: stay useful to employers between searches. Email rules
apply here too. CAN-SPAM makes no exception for business email ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)),
and Gmail requires SPF, DKIM and DMARC from senders of 5,000 or more messages a day ([Google](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126)).
This is not legal advice.

## Where we focus

- **Industry and role pages** that show your specialization to hiring managers and AI assistants.
- **LinkedIn and paid social campaigns** aimed at the titles that open job orders, kept separate from candidate ads.
- **Useful outreach and nurture**, such as hiring outlooks, for employers between searches.
- **Source tracking** from first touch to placement, so budget follows what fills roles.
