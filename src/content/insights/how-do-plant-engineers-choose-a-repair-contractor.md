Most plant engineers have formed a view of your company before they ever call. They've read your site, asked an AI assistant, checked a technical publication and talked to a colleague. By the time your phone rings, you may already be the favorite, or never on the list.

Here's what the latest research says about how that decision gets made, and what a repair or maintenance contractor can do about it.

## Do engineers research on their own first?

Yes, and for most of the journey. The 2026 State of Marketing to Engineers report from TREW Marketing and GlobalSpec, published in March 2026, found engineers spend 62% of the buying journey researching online.

B2B buyers in general look similar. The 6sense 2025 B2B Buyer Experience Report, a survey of nearly 4,000 buyers, found buyers made first contact with a seller about 61% of the way through their process. Buyers started the conversation 79% of the time. Neither study is about plants alone, but plant maintenance and reliability teams buy in much the same way.

## Where does AI fit in?

It's part of the process, not the final word. The same engineering report found 69% of technical buyers use generative AI during some part of a purchase. They rated their trust in AI answers 4.7 out of 10, up slightly from 4.4 the year before. So they check. Technical publications and vendor websites remain their top sources of product information.

That gives contractors two jobs. First, make sure an AI assistant has something clear and accurate to say about what you do: the repair methods, the assets, the conditions you work in, the standards you follow. Second, make sure your website confirms it in detail when the engineer goes to check. We call this Search Essence Optimization. Engineers aren't only searching to find you. They're searching to understand you.

## Does being known really matter that much?

It tips close calls. TREW and GlobalSpec found 53% of technical buyers said brand familiarity influenced their most recent purchase. 70% said they're more likely to choose the better-known brand when two solutions are technically similar.

For a repair contractor, "known" doesn't mean a big ad budget. It means the reliability engineer has seen your case write-ups, the maintenance manager remembers your crew from the last emergency, and procurement already has your paperwork.

## When does the shortlist form?

Very early. In the 6sense study, buyers filled about 3.6 shortlist spots on the first day of their search, and the eventual winner came from that day-one list 95% of the time. The first vendor contacted won 77% of the time.

Plant work adds a calendar to that. Planned repairs are usually scoped before the RFQ goes out, so an RFQ that lands in your inbox may already be shaped around someone else's method.

## Why is this worth getting right now?

Because downtime is expensive, and planned repair is how plants avoid it. Siemens' True Cost of Downtime 2024 report found a large plant still loses an average of 27 hours a month to unplanned downtime, down from 39 hours in 2019. Plants that can fix a failing tank or line on their own schedule, or while it stays in service, want a contractor they already trust.

## What should a repair contractor do about it?

Five steps, in this order.

1. **Make your methods easy to check.** One page per repair method and asset type, answering what engineers ask: can it be done in service, which standards apply, expected service life, time on site, what you need from the plant.
2. **Publish proof without names.** Short case write-ups that show the method, asset and conditions, without identifying the plant.
3. **Stay cleared.** Keep prequalification, safety records and insurance documents current, so a review never stalls an award.
4. **Get known before the outage.** A short, useful email to maintenance and reliability staff months ahead. Engineers prefer email to phone outreach, according to the report's 2026 findings. LinkedIn can put the same write-up in front of the right roles at your target plants.
5. **Measure awarded work.** Track walkdowns, bids and awards by source, so you know which effort earns planned jobs.
