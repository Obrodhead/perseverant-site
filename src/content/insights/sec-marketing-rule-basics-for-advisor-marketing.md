*This is a general overview for marketing planning, not legal advice. Your compliance team
or counsel should review any specific campaign.*

## What counts as an advertisement

Broadly, the rule covers communications that offer an adviser's services to prospective
clients or investors, including websites, social media, emails, ads and seminar materials.
That covers most of what a marketing firm produces for an advisory practice.

## The general prohibitions

The rule bars advertisements that include untrue statements of material fact, material
claims that cannot be substantiated, misleading implications, or a lopsided presentation
of benefits without fair treatment of risks.

## Testimonials, endorsements and ratings

Before the rule, testimonials were largely off limits. Now they are allowed with conditions,
including clear disclosure of whether the person is a client, whether they were compensated,
and any material conflicts of interest. Third-party ratings carry their own disclosure
requirements.

## Performance

Showing investment performance triggers detailed requirements, such as presenting net
performance alongside gross performance and showing standard time periods. Most marketing
pages are better off avoiding performance claims entirely unless compliance has built and
approved the presentation.

## How we build with this in mind

Every page and ad we draft is written for compliance review: no promises of returns, no
unsubstantiated superlatives and no testimonials without the required disclosures in place.
Content that earns AI citations tends to be educational anyway, which keeps it on safer
ground.
