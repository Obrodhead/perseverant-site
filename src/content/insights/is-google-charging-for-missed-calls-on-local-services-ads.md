For years, a missed call from a Local Services Ad cost you the job. Since October 1, 2026, it can cost you the job and the lead fee.

Google changed how it bills call leads. If someone calls during your business hours, nobody picks up, and they stay on the line more than 20 seconds, Google counts it as a valid lead and charges you for it. You don't have to answer, call back or ever speak to them.

This isn't a reason to panic or to turn your ads off. It's a reason to look hard at who answers your phone.

## What changed on October 1?

A missed call used to be free unless you followed up. Now the clock decides.

Google emailed Local Services Ads customers in late August under the subject line "Upcoming changes to lead charge policy," according to PPC Land and Search Engine Roundtable. TechWyse reported that before the change, a missed call with no voicemail only became billable if the business called the person back. Google's updated "How leads work" help page now lists three ways a call becomes a valid lead: you answer, you call back and speak with the customer, or you miss a call during business hours and the caller stays on more than 20 seconds.

Ring time alone doesn't trigger a charge on calls you do answer. Those are judged on the conversation, as before. The new rule is about calls that go nowhere.

## Does a call that reaches voicemail count?

Yes. This is the detail most coverage missed.

Google's help page says a call that reaches your voicemail or an automated greeting during business hours, without asking the caller to press a key, is treated as connected. If the caller stays on more than 20 seconds, it's charged. Leaving a message doesn't matter either way.

So voicemail is no longer a free buffer. A long greeting followed by "please leave a message" can now cost the same as a lead you actually talked to.

The one exception is a phone menu. If your system requires a key press to route the call, the 20-second timer starts after the first press. Callers who hang up without pressing anything aren't charged. We'd still be careful here. Adding a menu to dodge fees puts one more step between a ready buyer and a person. That trade rarely favors you.

## Which hours does Google count?

Your Google Business Profile hours. That makes them a billing setting now, not just a courtesy.

If your ad schedule differs from your listed hours, Google only charges unanswered calls during times covered by both. That gives you a simple control. If you list hours you can't staff, you're paying for calls nobody is there to take.

## Do you pay again when you call back?

No. Google says follow-up calls and messages with the same customer aren't charged again if there's been interaction within 15 days. Google Ads Liaison Ginny Marvin confirmed the 15-day rule publicly, according to Search Engine Roundtable.

Think about what that means. Once a missed call qualifies, the lead is already paid for. The callback is free. The only way to waste the money is to not call back, or to call back tomorrow after they've hired someone else.

## Can you get credited for bad leads?

Sometimes, and less by hand than before. Google says it reviews lead quality over 30 days and automatically credits leads it finds poor quality, duplicate or invalid. Search Engine Roundtable noted that the old list of non-creditable examples was largely removed from the help page in favor of this automated review.

You can still rate each lead in the feedback survey on the Leads page. Do it. Google says it uses that feedback to improve its models, and it's the only signal you send about lead quality.

One caution for health care. Google's help page says automatic credits and call recordings aren't available for U.S. health care categories or tax specialists. If you're in one of those, the missed-call change hits with fewer safety nets.

## Why is this really a speed-to-lead story?

Because Google just priced in something good operators already knew.

Google told advertisers the change is about meeting customer expectations while "rewarding businesses that provide excellent responsiveness," as TechWyse reported. That lines up with what Google already says about ranking. As we covered when [Local Services Ads started moving into Performance Max](/insights/local-services-ads-moving-to-performance-max/), Google lists responsiveness as a ranking factor and says missed calls can hurt it.

Put those together. A missed call now costs you the fee, the job and some of your ranking. An answered call earns all three back.

The person searching "roof leak repair near me" or "car accident lawyer" usually has a problem today. They tap the first good option. If it rings out or hits a recording, they tap the next one. That was true before October 1. The difference now is that you get an invoice for it.

## What should you fix this week?

Seven steps. Most take less than an hour.

1. **Match your profile hours to your staffed hours.** If nobody answers before 8 a.m. or after 5 p.m., don't list those hours.
2. **Line up your ad schedule.** Charges only apply when your ad schedule and listed hours overlap. Use that on purpose.
3. **Decide who answers when you can't.** A crew lead on a roof, an attorney in court and an intake desk at lunch all miss calls. A live answering service or a scheduled backup may cost less than a month of billed voicemails. Run the numbers for your volume.
4. **Shorten or rethink your voicemail.** If voicemail is your fallback, make it short and say when you'll call back. Better, route overflow to a person.
5. **Call back every missed lead within minutes.** The callback is free inside 15 days. The lead is already paid for.
6. **Rate your leads.** Use the feedback survey on the Leads page for every bad one.
7. **Mark October 1 in your reports.** Cost per lead before and after isn't comparable, because the definition of a lead changed. Judge the channel by [cost per booked appointment](/insights/cost-per-lead-vs-cost-per-appointment/) instead.

## What does this tell you about paid lead channels?

That the rules belong to the platform. Google changed what counts as a lead with about five weeks' notice, for every advertiser at once.

Local Services Ads can still be worth running for home service and legal businesses. They put you in front of people ready to hire. But a channel whose billing can change overnight shouldn't be your only source of new clients.

The steadier work is the same as always. Answer fast, with a person. Follow up until they book. And build demand you own, through a site and profile that help people and AI search understand who you are before they ever call. When they already know you, one missed call is a callback, not a lost job.
